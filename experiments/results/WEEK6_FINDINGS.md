# Week 6 — architecture (items #13–#17)

The schedule gated this week on "only if Weeks 1–2 show headroom". They did not,
so Week 6 is a **falsification exercise**: is there architectural headroom that
the recipe sweep could not reach? Every row below is an ablation, not a
deployment candidate.

All variants are generated by `experiments/arch.py` from `yolo11.yaml`, so they
are reproducible from the shipped ultralytics with no third-party fork. COCO
weights are transferred for every shape-matching layer, so the only randomly
initialised parameters are the blocks being ablated. All trained **80 epochs /
patience 20** on `data/mixed_pictor_sh17`, and `none` — the stock yolo11s neck
through the identical code path — is the control.

## Items #13 and #14 — P2 head and neck attention

| variant | params M | GFLOPs | mAP50 | Δ mAP | **W+WH** | **Δ W+WH** | lat ms | Δ lat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `none` (control) | 9.429 | 21.67 | 0.4616 | — | 0.4099 | — | 5.88 | — |
| `p2` | 9.576 | **29.07** | 0.4616 | +0.000 | 0.4212 | +0.011 | 6.76 | +0.88 |
| `cbam` | 9.775 | 21.68 | 0.4596 | −0.002 | 0.4309 | +0.021 | 6.39 | +0.51 |
| `se` | 9.472 | 21.68 | 0.4857 | **+0.024** | 0.4225 | +0.013 | 6.25 | +0.37 |
| `eca` | 9.429 | 21.68 | 0.4157 | −0.046 | 0.4073 | −0.003 | 6.17 | +0.29 |
| `coordatt` | 9.463 | 21.68 | 0.4510 | −0.011 | **0.4547** | **+0.045** | 6.69 | +0.81 |

`W+WH` is the mean AP50 over the two well-supported test classes (456 and 517
instances). The `mAP50` column averages those with `WHV` (20 instances) and `WV`
(6) and should not be read on its own — see `GAINS.md` §3.1.

### Findings

1. **`mAP50` and `W+WH` disagree on the ranking, in both directions.** `se` is
   best on mAP50 (+0.024) and fourth on W+WH; `coordatt` is *negative* on mAP50
   (−0.011) and best on W+WH by a clear margin (+0.045). The disagreement is the
   thin classes moving on 20 and 6 objects. Anyone quoting the mAP50 column here
   would adopt the wrong block.

2. **The P2 head buys nothing worth its cost.** `mAP50` is identical to the
   control to four decimals; on the well-supported classes it is +0.011, for
   **+34% GFLOPs** (29.07 vs 21.67) and +15% latency. Item #13's hypothesis was
   that Pictor's distant workers are resolution-starved at P3/8. Week 1 already
   falsified the same hypothesis from the other direction — imgsz 960 and 1280
   both lost to 640. Two independent routes to the same answer: these workers
   are context-starved, not resolution-starved.

3. **Attention is within noise, with one exception worth a second look.**
   Four of the five variants land inside a 0.024 band on W+WH (0.407–0.431)
   around a 0.410 control — that is the spread you get from reordering the same
   experiment. `coordatt` sits 0.024 above the next best, which is the only
   effect in this table larger than the spread of the others.

   **This is one seed on one dataset and should not be adopted on this
   evidence.** If it is worth pursuing, the next step is a 3-seed repeat of
   `none` vs `coordatt` alone; the mechanism is at least plausible, since
   coordinate attention keeps *where* a feature is and helmet evidence is
   positionally constrained to the top of a person box. Everything else in this
   table is a null result.

4. **None of it competes with the data change.** The best architectural effect
   here is +0.045 on a class-mean AP. Week 7's data change moved held-out verdict
   accuracy 0.684 → 0.803 and violation recall 0.457 → 0.515 on the *same*
   architecture. The deployed checkpoint uses the stock yolo11s neck.

## Item #15 — the two-stage cascade, costed

`w6_two_stage`. Amsar's shipped design is a person detector, then a crop, then a
PPE classifier. A single-pass model is O(1) in worker count; a cascade is O(n).

| workers in frame | single-pass ms | cascade ms | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7.30 | 7.55 | 1.03× |
| 3 | 7.30 | 8.03 | 1.10× |
| 10 | 7.30 | 9.72 | 1.33× |
| 40 | 7.30 | 16.95 | 2.32× |

**The cascade is already more expensive at one worker in frame** and degrades
linearly. Pictor test averages 3.16 workers per image.

The stage-2 model here is a 3-conv stub — a deliberate lower bound. A real PPE
classifier is slower, so the crossover is optimistic *for the cascade*. The
association loop is 0.06 ms and is not the bottleneck; the second forward pass is.

## Item #17 — quantisation

`w6_qat`. QAT is **not run**, deliberately: it needs fake-quant observers in the
training loop and exists to recover accuracy that the variants above show there
is no architectural headroom for. PTQ is what gates deployment, and is measured.

TensorRT FP16/INT8 could not be measured on this machine — the `tensorrt` package
is absent and `onnxruntime-gpu` fails against this CUDA build (missing
`cublasLt64_13.dll`). Those are the rows that matter on a Jetson and remain
unmeasured here; `bench_jetson.py` exists for the real numbers.

## Item #16 — baselines

`w6_baselines` (RT-DETR) is queued. D-FINE and RTMDet are not in ultralytics
8.4.127 and would need a separate framework, so the "baseline table" is narrower
than the schedule imagined.

## Verdict on Week 6

**No architectural change is adopted.** The schedule's own gate — "only if Weeks
1–2 show headroom" — was the right call: the P2 head is a measurable cost for no
gain, and attention is within run-to-run spread except for one variant that needs
a seed-repeat before anyone believes it. The project's conclusion is unchanged
and now tested from a third direction: **the data is the lever.**
